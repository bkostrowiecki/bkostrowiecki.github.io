It seems like slop bros try to convince everyone that coding is dead. It's not like that yet, but we're heading there. But they missing the point, that creating software and programming was never about coding.

At the begining knowing the hardware layer was necessary to program computers. Going through years people created more and more layers of abstraction. Programmers invented drivers, higher-level languages, database to solve common issues. It was all done to make things more stable, more accesible and so more people may actually solve real problems. But it was never about code itself. It was always about forcing computer to do things to the own taste of programmers and designers.

That's actually where AI can really shine: if it's used as a tool to implement the will of the designer. But most of things that are created using AI have no human touch at all. Cons of AI coding still are actually related to that one specific reason.

Next-gen models halucinate even more then previous generation. It seems like the halucantions are now directed torwards halucinating more code. Usually it halucinates requirements that were not specified by designer.

I'm usually not happy when I ask a model to do very specific thing and it does 5 more things, probably because it was teached with the phrase which some project managers like to use: underpromise and overdeliver. It creates a bloat which makes everything much harder to mantain.

In the same time they tend to ignore with time even simplest overall instructions, like: use the UI framework to do something instead of coding it from scratch. So I need to constantly remind the model how it should proceed so it's approach will be in tact with other requirements.

All of it looks like the shadiest conflict of interests in the world: the companies behind AI wants me to spend more money on their tools. Meanwhile, I would like to spend as few dimes as possible to make things work. They come up with ideas that helps me estimate my costs, but it seems like their have no interest in tackling the core reason behind it: overgeneration of code.

Most of the tools that were created over decades to help with programming shinked down number of the decisions that programmer needs to make to the most important parts. Those decisions that left are usually crucial to solve some very specific problem. Otherwise, it would have been solved long time ago with another levels of abstraction.

Still programmers need to know implementation details. Many of technologies over decades were adveristed as black boxes. But to operate smoothly programmers needed to know implementation details anyway. With AI we get real black box that we cannot look inside.

At the end of the day, the most important part of the programmer's job is to make decisions to solve the problem in the best possible way. It requires knowledge about technology, bussiness, human behavior.

...and it seems like slop bros try to convince the world that the power of AI is to minimize people's decision making, so we can all grab a cup of tea and watch as the same software being generated.

The sad thing is the quality of work without human input is bad. And I don't mean code itself. I saw ThreeJS demos of the newest Fable 5.2 model created by some slop bro. It looks really bad, but the slop bro was really proud of it, like mommies are proud of what they children drew in the kindergarden's class.

That's what AI actually made to the market: people started to accept really bad quality of work just because something was created by AI. It's funny, how the first technology that actually make a regress on quality appears to be here to overtake everyone's job.
